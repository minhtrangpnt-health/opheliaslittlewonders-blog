Asked by kids everywhere, and by Milo at the beach.

How long can a whale hold its breath?

Longer than a movie. Longer than two movies, actually.

Kid: “How long can I hold my breath?”
Grown-up: “About as long as it takes to say ‘please don’t try that in the bath’.”

From Just Imagine! Could You Hold Your Breath for Almost Four Hours?

The champion diver

A Cuvier’s beaked whale holds the record. Scientists following 23 of these whales with special tags, off the coast of North Carolina in the USA, once recorded one staying under for 222 minutes. That is almost four hours on one breath.

Under the ice

A Weddell seal lives in icy Antarctica. It dives under the sea ice to hunt and can stay down for more than half an hour. Then it finds a hole in the ice to pop up and breathe.

How do they do it?

Their bodies store lots of oxygen in their muscles, like a backpack full of air.

Your superpower is different

People need to breathe all the time, every few seconds, even when we sleep. That’s why, when you swim, you come up for air often, and a grown-up always watches.

Try this

Whale blow. Stand up tall, take a big breath in, and blow it all out at once with a loud “PFOOSH!”, like a whale coming up for air. Who in the family can make the biggest whale blow?

Ask your kids (or your class)

What would you ask a whale about its day?
What would its underwater picnic look like?
What would you draw on a whale-sized watch?

Stay safe: Never play breath-holding games in the water, not even in the bath. Always swim with a grown-up watching, between the flags at the beach.
