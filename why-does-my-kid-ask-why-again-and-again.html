For parents · what research says

Why does my kid ask “why?” again and again?

It can feel like a test of patience. Research says it is something else.

1 to 3questions a minute, when young children chat with a familiar grown-up at home

So many questions

Psychologist Michele Chouinard listened to more than 200 hours of recordings of four preschoolers talking with grown-ups at home, about 25,000 questions in all. At home with a familiar adult, the children asked one to three questions each minute. About two-thirds of them were questions to get information.

Around age 3, about a quarter of their questions asked for an explanation: the “why” and “how” kind.

Why the same question comes back

Researchers Brandy Frazier, Susan Gelman and Henry Wellman studied children aged 2 to 5. When a child got a real explanation, they usually seemed satisfied: they agreed, or asked a new follow-up question. When the answer was not an explanation, they were more likely to ask the same question again, or offer their own explanation.

As the researchers put it, when preschoolers ask “why”, they are “trying to get to the bottom of things”.

What this means for us

The second “why?” is often not a game, and not ignoring you. It can mean the first answer did not explain enough yet.

It is tiring, and that’s normal

At 6 pm, after a long day, the fifth “but why?” can feel like too much. Feeling worn out doesn’t make you a bad parent. Nobody has a perfect answer every time.

One small real reason

“Because the sun’s light bounces around in the air” works better than “because it just is”. Short is fine, as long as it explains something.

“Let’s find out together”

Not knowing is OK. Looking it up later, in a book or online with your child, still counts as an answer.

“Ask me again at dinner”

When you are out of energy, park the question kindly. Write it on a scrap of paper so it doesn’t get lost.

“What do you think?”

Kids often have their own theory. Hearing it tells you what kind of explanation they are looking for.

Your turn

What question did your kid ask three times this week? Tell us, and how you finally answered it.

Sources: ASCD Educational Leadership, “What Children Learn from Questioning”; Frazier, Gelman and Wellman (2009), PubMed, “Preschoolers’ search for explanatory information within adult-child conversation”.
