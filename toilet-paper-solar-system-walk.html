Walk the solar system with a roll of toilet paper (ages 5-8)

A long hallway, one roll, and a lot of very surprised faces at the far end.

100sheets

of toilet paper take you from the Sun to Pluto, if Earth sits just two and a half sheets away.

Set it up

Find a long hallway or a stretch of playground. Put a paper plate at one end for the Sun. Unroll the toilet paper from the plate, counting sheets out loud together. Children hold cards for the eight planets and a separate card for Pluto, a dwarf planet, at the right sheets. These counts come from a NASA activity.

The whole walk, with the Sun peeking in at the start. Mercury, Venus, Earth and Mars all squeeze into the first 4 sheets.

Zooming in on the first 4 sheets. The planets are drawn roughly the right size compared with each other. The Sun is far too big to fit.

The big surprise

Before you start, ask everyone to guess where Jupiter will be, and mark the guesses with sticky notes. Then count it out and see who was close. The four planets closest to the Sun crowd together near the plate. After Mars comes a very long walk of empty space before Jupiter, and an even longer one out to Neptune and Pluto.

Jobs for every age

Little ones can be the counters, shouting each sheet number. Older ones hold the planet cards and wave when the count reaches them. Someone who loves being in charge can be the Sun and stay put, which is the hardest job of all.

Ask the group

If you lived on Neptune, how long do you think it would take to walk to the Sun?

Sources: NASA Out-of-School Learning Network, “Earth and Space Explorers” (toilet paper solar system); NASA Night Sky Network, “Pocket Solar System”; NASA, “Planetary Fact Sheet” (planet sizes).
