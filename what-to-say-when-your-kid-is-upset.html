For parents

What to say when your kid is very, very upset (ages 3-8)

Hint: “Calm down!” has never once calmed anyone down.

Grown-up: “Use your words.”
Kid: “AAAAARGH.”
Grown-up: “Those are technically sounds.”

Five small steps

The Raising Children Network suggests five steps: notice the feeling, name it, pause, give support, and then deal with the problem.

Naming it can sound like: “I can see you’re feeling angry about turning off the TV.” This shows your child you understand, and that the feeling is OK even if the behaviour isn’t.

Why pause?

Telling an upset child to use their words or take deep breaths often doesn’t work in the moment. Raising Children suggests waiting instead: stay close, keep everyone safe, and count slowly to five in your head. Talk about what happened once they’re calm.

Game: how does it feel in my body?

At a calm time, pick a feeling and act it out. Angry: stomp and scrunch. Worried: wobbly tummy. Happy: bouncy feet. Kids aged 3 to 7 can guess your feeling, then show you theirs. Practising when everyone is relaxed is what Raising Children suggests.
