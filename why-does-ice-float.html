Asked by kids everywhere, usually just before bedtime.

Why does ice float?

The frozen bit takes up more room than you might expect.

Kid: “The ice is sitting on the water.”
Grown-up: “It is floating.”
Kid: “A very cold seat.”

  • Ice is less dense than liquid water
  • Freezing spreads water molecules apart
  • Lakes can freeze from the top

The same material, more space

Ice floats because it is less dense than liquid water.

  • “Less dense” means there is less mass packed into the same amount of space.
  • The University of Idaho explains that water is unusual in being less dense as a solid than as a liquid.
  • Yale University explains that ice has a more open arrangement of molecules, occupying more room for the same mass.

That matters beyond a drink with ice cubes.

  • Because ice floats, lakes can freeze at the top while liquid water remains underneath.
  • The university explains why this is important for living things below the ice.
  • A floating solid layer is part of a much bigger winter story than a cold cube in a kitchen bowl.

The wow fact is that freezing water changes its arrangement enough to affect floating.

  • “It is solid” does not automatically tell us it must sink.
  • Nor does one ice cube tell us whether every other object will float.
  • We need to think about the particular material and liquid involved.

Game: the imaginary crowded room

Hold both hands comfortably with fingers close together.

  • Pretend they show a group standing closely packed.
  • Now spread the fingers gently, giving the same pretend group more room.
  • Ask what changed about the space occupied.

Let another person narrate the two arrangements.

  • This is a model of spacing, not an exact picture of water molecules.
  • Your fingers are not freezing, and they cannot demonstrate all the forces inside ice.

If you notice ice during an ordinary supervised meal, describe what is above and below the water.

  • There is no need to handle very cold objects, put small cubes in mouths or visit frozen water outdoors.
  • Our hand model can be the whole activity today.
